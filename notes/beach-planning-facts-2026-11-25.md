## Rip currents
- CONFIRMED https://www.weather.gov/safety/ripcurrent-science ("Rip Current Science"): a rip current is a strong, narrow seaward flow from near shore through the surf zone and beyond; not undertow or a tidal current. Neither rips nor undertow pull you under. Fighting straight back to shore can exhaust even strong swimmers. Flow can pulse and more than double in speed. Signs: darker narrow gap between breaking waves, choppy water, sand/foam moving seaward. Best protection: swim near a lifeguard.
- CONFIRMED https://www.weather.gov/safety/ripcurrent ("Rip Current Safety"): swim where lifeguards are on duty (cites USLA odds 1 in 18 million drowning at a guarded beach); check the local beach forecast and ask the lifeguard; rips often form on calm sunny days; do not turn your back on the ocean.
